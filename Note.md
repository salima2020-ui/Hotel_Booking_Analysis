## Business Insights for Hotel Revenue Management

1. **City Hotel's cancellation rate is 1.5x higher than Resort Hotel's (41.7% vs 27.8%).** This is linked to City Hotel bookings having a longer average lead time (110 days vs 92 days). For revenue management: apply stricter deposit/prepayment policies on longer-lead-time City Hotel bookings, and consider a separate overbooking strategy per hotel rather than a single blended assumption.

2. **Cancellation risk rises sharply as lead time increases.** The longer in advance a booking is made, the more likely the guest's plans change before arrival. For revenue management: require a partial non-refundable deposit once lead time exceeds roughly 90 days, while keeping more flexible terms for last-minute bookings (0-7 days).

3. **Demand is highly seasonal and differs by hotel type.** August is the peak month (~13.9k bookings) and January the lowest (~5.9k); Resort Hotel demand is heavily concentrated in the summer months (June-August), while City Hotel demand stays comparatively steady year-round. For revenue management: apply more aggressive summer/winter rate differences for the Resort Hotel, and use off-season packages (Nov-Feb) to boost demand rather than blanket discounting.

4. **Online TA is both the largest booking channel and the one with the highest cancellation rate**, while Corporate and Direct segments bring in lower volume but far more reliable (low-cancellation) bookings. For revenue management: negotiate stricter cancellation terms on OTA-sourced rate plans, and invest in growing Direct and Corporate channels — through loyalty incentives or negotiated corporate rates — to increase the share of "sticky," low-cancellation revenue.
